The 2027 Consumer Confidence Report changes, for water systems under 10,000 people

First report under the new rule: due July 1, 2027. Still once a year under 10,000 people.

EPA revised the Consumer Confidence Report (CCR) rule for community water systems. The revised rule applies from January 1, 2027, so the first report under it is the one due July 1, 2027. Systems serving under 10,000 people still deliver once a year. What changes for them: a summary at the top of the report, delivery methods spelled out in the rule, and a new standard paragraph.

The revision was published on May 24, 2024 (Federal Register 2024-10919, 89 FR 45980). The rule is 40 CFR part 141, subpart O.

Quotes are from eCFR, checked November 10, 2026. Your state may adopt the rule on its own schedule or add requirements; check with your state drinking water program. This page isn't legal advice.

Which version applies when

§ 141.152(a) sets three periods:

PeriodRule text that applies
Until December 31, 2026§§ 141.151-141.155 as codified on July 1, 2023 (the old rule)
From January 1, 2027§§ 141.151-141.156 as codified on July 1, 2024 (the CCR revision), with one exception in § 141.153
From November 1, 2027§§ 141.151-141.156 as codified on July 1, 2025

The November 2027 step brings in text added after July 2024, including the lead wording in § 141.154(d) that starts "Lead can cause serious health effects in people of all ages". Reports due July 1, 2027 follow the July 1, 2024 text.

Checklist: what changes from 2027

  • Summary at the top. "Each report must include a summary displayed prominently at the beginning of the report, including a brief description of the nature of the report" (§ 141.156(a)). At a minimum it summarises violations and compliance information, and gives contact details for the owner, operator or designee (§ 141.156(b)).
  • Standard sharing paragraph. The report includes this text: "Please share this information with anyone who drinks this water (or their guardians), especially those who may not have received this report directly (for example, people in apartments, nursing homes, schools, and businesses). You can do this by posting this report in a public place or distributing copies by hand, mail, email, or another method." (§ 141.156)
  • Delivery. Use at least one of: mail or hand deliver a paper copy; mail a notice with a direct link to the report on a website; email a direct link or an electronic version; or another direct method your state approves in writing (§ 141.155(a)(1)).
  • Paper on request. If you deliver electronically, you must give a paper copy to any customer who asks, and the notice must prominently say how to ask (§ 141.155(a)(2)).
  • Certification. Within 10 days of the delivery deadline, send your state a copy of the report and a certification "that the report(s) has/have been distributed to customers, and that the information is correct and consistent with the compliance monitoring data previously submitted to the primacy agency" (§ 141.155(c)).

Mailing waiver for systems under 10,000

Your Governor or their designee can waive direct delivery for systems serving fewer than 10,000 people (§ 141.155(g)). A system with a waiver must publish the report in a local newspaper or local online news site, tell customers it won't be mailed, and make it available on request. Systems serving 500 or fewer can instead give notice at least once a year, by mail, door-to-door or by posting, that the report is available on request. Whether your state grants the waiver is up to your state.

Questions

When does the revised Consumer Confidence Report rule start?

January 1, 2027 (40 CFR 141.152(a)). The first report under it is the one due to customers by July 1, 2027.

Does a small water system have to send the report twice a year?

No. Only systems serving 10,000 or more persons must distribute it twice per calendar year (40 CFR 141.155(j)(2)). Every system delivers by July 1 each year (141.155(j)(1)).

Can we email the report or post it online?

Yes. Mailing a notice with a direct link to the report on a website, or emailing a link or an electronic version, are allowed delivery methods (40 CFR 141.155(a)(1)). You must give a paper copy to any customer who asks, and a report posted online must stay available for at least 3 years.

When is the certification due?

No later than 10 days after the date you must distribute the report. You send your state a copy of the report and a certification that it was distributed and that the information is correct (40 CFR 141.155(c)).

What has to be in the new summary?

A brief description of the nature of the report, a summary of violations and compliance information, and contact details for the owner, operator or designee, displayed prominently at the beginning of the report (40 CFR 141.156).

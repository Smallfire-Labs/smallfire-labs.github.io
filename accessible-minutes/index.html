What we fix, and what we don't

What we do

  • Text-based meeting minutes and agendas
  • Headings, numbered and lettered items, reading order, document title and language
  • Logos and seals kept, with a text description
  • Web and email links kept
  • Simple tables, such as a roll call

What we don't do

  • Scanned pages (no text layer)
  • Fillable forms
  • Full agenda packets, flyers, newsletters and other designed documents
  • Tables beyond simple ones
  • Public notices (not tested yet)
  • Screen-reader testing, or any certification

The returned file is a new PDF in a plain, re-typeset layout: the words and numbers are the same, but fonts, spacing and page breaks differ from your original. Running page headers and page numbers are removed, and the report lists them. Two-column layouts and watermarks take extra hand work; if we add a word (such as a column heading), the report says which word and why.

Questions

Will this make our website ADA compliant?

We can't promise that. veraPDF PDF/UA-1 is a machine check: it can't judge whether a text description is good or whether the reading order makes sense. Whether your site meets WCAG 2.1 Level AA is for you to judge. This isn't legal advice.

Do we need to fix our old minutes?

Mostly not. Under the Justice Department's rule, PDFs posted before your compliance date are exempt unless people still use them to apply for or take part in your services. Minutes and agendas you post after that date are covered. Dates and exceptions: which town PDFs must be accessible.

Do you carry insurance?

No. If your purchasing rules require a vendor's certificate of insurance, we can't take the job yet.

We download the public files you link to, keep them and our versions in a folder for your order only, and delete them 90 days after we send you the finished files. You can ask us to delete them sooner. See the privacy policy.
